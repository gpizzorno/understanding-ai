# AI-Assisted Work: Scenarios

*Printable handout for the [AI-Assisted Work](https://gpizzorno.github.io/understanding-ai/unit_2/ai_assisted_work.html) activity.*

**For each scenario:**

1. Decide, before discussing: is this acceptable? Under what conditions?
2. Is the AI acting as a tool, a source, or both? What would you want to check before relying on the result? What, if anything, should be said about the AI's role, and to whom?
3. What would change your answer? A different discipline, a different audience, more checking, a different tool?

**Four lenses to structure your discussion:** source or tool? · light assistance or substantive contribution? · private or public? · what does the relevant policy say?

## Scenario 1: Brainstorming Search Terms

A historian asks *ChatGPT* for alternative search terms to use in a newspaper database when researching nineteenth-century discussions of emigration. They use some of the suggestions to improve their archive searches, but none of the AI-generated language appears in the final publication.

## Scenario 2: A Summary as a Thinking Aid

A researcher uploads three letters to Gemini Notebook and asks for a summary of recurring themes. They then write their own paragraph for an article, using the summary only as a thinking aid, and check the themes directly against the letters.

## Scenario 3: AI-Written Prose in an Essay

A student asks *Claude* to draft two paragraphs comparing two primary sources. The student lightly edits the wording and submits the essay under their own name, without mentioning the AI.

## Scenario 4: A Bibliography from an LLM

A researcher asks an LLM to generate a bibliography of scholarship on Victorian correspondence networks. Several of the citations look plausible, and the researcher includes them in a handout without checking them.

## Scenario 5: A Translation Draft

A researcher uses an LLM to produce a first-pass translation of a short letter in French, then revises it carefully against the original, consults a dictionary, and notes the ambiguous phrases.

## Scenario 6: An AI-Generated Dataset

A researcher asks an LLM to extract all the person names from 200 letters, and puts the results into a spreadsheet, which becomes a network visualization in a conference presentation. Only a handful of rows are spot-checked.
